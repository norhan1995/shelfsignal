# Two-year thesis

Over the next two years, AI interfaces will move from places where people ask for information to surfaces that notice a consequential decision, compress the evidence, and prepare the next reversible move. The durable product is not a smarter answer box. It is a role-specific decision loop with an event trigger, typed intent, evidence, policy, action staging, human approval, and a learning signal when the operator corrects it.

The interface should become quieter as confidence rises and more explicit as uncertainty or consequence rises. In a replenishment workflow, that means the system watches every SKU but asks a manager to look at only the exception whose cost of waiting is highest. It can prepare a purchase order, but it cannot turn a prediction into a commitment without the person who owns the consequence.

This pattern will spread to support, finance, compliance, and engineering. The winning products will not be chatbots hidden inside dashboards; they will be accountable decision surfaces that make the next action legible, reversible, and fast.
